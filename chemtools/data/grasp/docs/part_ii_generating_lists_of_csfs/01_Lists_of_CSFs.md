### 5.1
### Congurations, conguration state functions
A conguration is a number of orbitals with occupation numbers, e.g. 1s22s2(2p-)2, 1s22s2(2p-)2p, 1s22s22p2, where we use the notation 1s, 2s, 2p-, 2p for 1s1/2, 2s1/2, 2p1/2, 2p3/2. Frequently, the non-relativistic notation is used instead 1s22s22p2. Conguration state functions (CSFs) are formed by angular couplings of the orbitals in a congu- ration. Depending on the structure of the conguration, i.e. number of open shells, there may be many angular couplings and thus CSFs for each conguration. An angular coupling is sometimes referred to as a coupling tree. In Grasp2018 the CSFs are given in rcsf.inp. The CSFs comprise three lines in the le. The rst line gives the conguration and lines two and three dene the coupling tree. The CSFs are ordered in blocks specied by parity and J symmetry, the blocks being separated by an asterisk ∗. ### 5.2
### Multireference
The starting point for a study is normally a calculation for a number of important CSFs that dene the multireference (MR). The CSFs in the MR are those that can be formed from nearly degenerate congurations, see [24] chapter 4.1 The wave function based on the CSFs in the MR is the rst approximation and it is the starting point for further renements. The concept of an MR is best illustrated by some examples. Suppose we want to compute the wave function for the ground state 3s2 1S0 of Mg I. The 3s2, 3p2 and 3d2 congurations are formed by orbitals with the same principal quantum numbers and the congurations are closely degenerate. An MR in this case could consist of the CSFs that can be formed from these congurations. Suppose we want to compute the wave functions for the 3s3p 3P0,1,2, 1P1 excited states of Mg I. The 3s3p and 3p3d congurations are formed by orbitals with the same principal quantum numbers and these congurations are closely degenerate. An MR in this case could consist of the CSFs that can be formed from these congurations. However, it turns out that 3s4p is important and thus a more suitable MR should consist of CSFs also from the latter conguration. We want to compute the wave functions for the states of 2s22p2 and 2s2p3. The 2s22p2 and 2p4 congurations are formed by orbitals with the same principal quantum numbers and the congurations are closely degenerate. Thus the MR for the even states would consist of the CSFs that can be formed from these two congurations. It turns out that the 2s2p23d and 2s23d2 congurations are important and a better MR includes CSFs also from these congurations. Looking at 2s2p3 there is no other conguration of the same parity that can be formed by orbitals with the same principal quantum numbers. In this case the MR would consist of CSFs formed from this single conguration. But, also 2p33d, 2s22p3d and 2s2p3d2 are important and the MR should consist of CSFs also from these congurations. We see that the selection of the MR is far from trivial and it often requires a number of exploratory calculations to nd a good MR. The program rcsfmr, described in section 7.6, is designed to support the exploratory process. For 'all levels' calculations or spectrum calculations, see section 4.7, where wave functions are determined for a number of states belonging to several congurations the MR is often taken as the set of CSFs that can be formed from these congurations. Suppose that we want to determine the wave functions for states of the 3l3l′, 3l4l′ and 3l5s congurations in Mg-like ions. The MR in this case would just be the CSFs that can be formed from these congurations. If we do the calculations by parity, the MR for the even parity states would be the CSFs formed from even parity congurations and the MR for the odd parity states would be the CSFs formed from odd parity congurations.
### 5.3
### Active set approach
CSFs are often generated using the active set approach. In the active set approach CSFs of a specied parity and J symmetry are obtained from angular couplings of congurations generated by excitations from orbitals of one or more congurations in the MR to orbitals in an active set (AS). The active set consists of the active orbitals in the ref- erence conguration together with orbitals up to a given limit specied by the highest principal quantum number of each orbital symmetry. Closed orbitals are fully occupied and make up the core. No excitations are allowed from inactive orbitals of the reference conguration. Excitations are allowed from the active orbitals of the reference conguration to orbitals in the active set. Excitations from active orbitals having minimal occupations are such that the occupations after the excitations are always larger or equal to the specied minimal occupation. Based on perturbation theory one can show that the major electron correlation eects are cap- tured by including, in the ASF, the CSFs that can be formed from congurations obtained by allowing single (S) and double (D) excitations from the most important congurations, dening the multireference (MR), to an extended active set of orbitals [24]. For small systems, e.g. nominal three and four electron systems, it is sometimes advantageous to include CSFs that can be formed from all possible excitations: single (S), double (D), triple (T), (Q) quadruple, etc. This expansion is referred to as the complete active space (CAS).

### Dierent types of correlation eects

Often excitations are done only from outer orbitals and the corresponding CSFs are said to describe valence-valence correlation. If one excitation is from a core orbital and one from an outer orbital then the corresponding CSFs are said to describe core-valence correlation. If both excitations are from the core the corresponding CSFs are said to describe core-core correlation. As an example of correlation eects we look at the ground state of Mg 1s22s22p63s2 1S0. ### 5.5
### Doubly occupied correlation orbitals
Accounting for electron correlation eects including core-core often leads to very large expansions. Imposing the restriction that correlation orbitals are doubly occupied reduces the expansion size. For example, if 3s, 4s, 5s, 3p-, 3p, 4p-, 4p, 5p-, 5p, 3d-, 3d, 4d-, 4d, 5d-, 5d are correlation orbitals in relativistic notation only excitation pairs 3s2, 4s2, 5s2, (3p-)2, (3p-)3p, 3p2, (4p-)2, (4p-)4p, 4p2 etc are allowed. Such an expansion still describes a fair part of the correlation. A practical example of how to use the restriction that correlation orbitals are doubly occupied is given in the sixth example of the next chapter.
### 5.6
### CSFs interacting with the MR
It is important to realize that the active set approach, as we have described it above, is based on generation of congurations that are then coupled to form CSFs. However, not all CSFs generated in this way have non-zero Hamiltonian matrix elements (interact) with CSFs in the MR. Generated CSFs not interacting with the CSFs of the MR can, without major loss of accuracy, be removed from the list of CSFs [24]. This is done by the program rcsfinteract. The reduction of CSFs is important mainly for complex systems, where the list of CSFs grow very rapidly with the increasing active set of orbitals.
